OBSERVER'S NOTES

Real science.
A model of the cosmos.

These are illustrative single-star evolutionary tracks, not predictions for a particular observed star. A solar mass is the mass of our Sun.

Gravity gathers gas into a protostar. Fusion supports a main-sequence star. Later, its initial mass, composition, mass loss and companions shape its fate. The 15- and 30-solar-mass tracks show possible neutron-star and black-hole outcomes; mass alone does not guarantee either result. Some black holes form without a bright supernova.

Each timeline segment has equal screen space, but stages last very different lengths of time. Ages are approximate teaching examples. Remnants can persist far beyond this timeline. Sizes, brightness and particle speeds are adjusted for visibility; nebula colours are illustrative. The black hole's glowing disk represents nearby hot gas, if present. Cloud turbulence, flare loops, and expanding shock fronts are procedural illustrations with accelerated motion. The black-hole halo suggests bent disk light; it is not a full relativity simulation. The brighter approaching side illustrates Doppler beaming.
